## The exam

- 200-301, blueprint v1.1. 120 minutes, about 100 questions (multiple choice, drag-and-drop, and simulation-style items). Booked through Pearson VUE; check the current price when you book.
- Cisco doesn't publish an official passing score, so aim well above "just enough".
- A CCNA is a CCNA regardless of blueprint version; it's valid for three years.

## The daily loop (about 1.5–2 hrs)

1. **Anki reviews first** (20–45 min; grows over the semester). Reviews before new material, every day.
2. **Lecture** (about 20–40 min). Take notes in the day's note under _Key ideas_, in your own words. No slide screenshots.
3. **Lab** (about 20–40 min). Do it without watching the solution first. Copy every command you used into _Commands from the lab_ with a plain-English meaning, and into [[02 Command Reference]].
4. **New Anki cards** for that day. Grade honestly: if you had to guess, it's **Again**.
5. **Subnetting drill** (5–10 min, from Week 3 on). Aim for fast, accurate answers on /24–/30 problems.
6. Tick Lecture / Lab / Anki in the tracker.

**Gate rule:** don't start the next day until lecture, lab, and flashcards are all done. If a lab didn't click, redo it from a blank Packet Tracer file before moving on.

**Bad week?** Protect Anki first, then catch up on lectures and labs over the weekend. Skipping reviews creates a backlog that snowballs.

## Anki setup

- Import each day's deck when you reach it, then move the cards into one master deck (e.g. `CCNA::Master`) so reviews come up mixed.
- Only press **Good** when you actually knew it. Mis-grading hides weak spots until Boson or exam day.
- Consider turning on FSRS in deck options. It schedules reviews more efficiently with the same honest grading.
- Log each day's review count in the tracker to keep the streak visible.

**Boson rules**

- The **first attempt** on each exam is your real score. Retakes inflate because you remember questions, so the tracker flags them.
- Read the explanation for every question, including ones you got right by guessing.
- Suggested readiness bar (my suggestion, not an official number): first-attempt Exam D in the high 80s or better, with no domain lagging far behind.
- Not there by Jan 10? Use custom mode on weak domains and consider moving the exam later in January. Keep at least one retake window before Feb 2.

## NetSim plan

The author of the video you're following bought NetSim and only did three labs. To avoid that:

- **When to buy:** around the Nov 16 checkpoint, when you've covered enough of the course for its labs to be useful. Check Boson for ExSim + NetSim bundle pricing.
- **How to use:** targeted, not start-to-finish. Each week in Phase 3, pick 3–5 labs in your weakest Boson domain. Heavier on configuration topics: VLANs/trunking, STP, EtherChannel, OSPF, ACLs, NAT, port security.
- **Rule:** do each lab without hints first. Log the result in the tracker and debrief tricky ones in `NetSim/`.
- Optional during Phase 1: if a Jeremy's lab didn't click even after redoing it, a NetSim lab on the same topic is a good third rep.

## Phase 4: Exam week (Jan 12–16)

- 2–3 days before: light Anki, review [[01 Weak Areas]] and [[02 Command Reference]], one short custom quiz. No new material.
- Day before: confirm appointment time and ID requirements with Pearson VUE. If testing online, run the system check early.
- During the exam: you can't go back to earlier questions on Cisco exams, so don't burn time. Answer, flag it mentally, and move on.

## Contingencies

- **Fail on the first attempt:** debrief the score report by domain the same day. You must wait 5 days before retesting. Rebook immediately, then target the weak domains with NetSim and Boson custom mode.
- **Slip past Feb 2:** you'll be on v2.0. Most of the fundamentals carry over, but v2.0 adds troubleshooting-level questions, so lab time matters even more. Re-plan around updated materials.
